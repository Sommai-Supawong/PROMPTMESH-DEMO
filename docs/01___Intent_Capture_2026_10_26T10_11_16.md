
# 01 — Intent Capture

## 1. Intent Statement

Build a **Hyperlocal Disaster-to-Commerce (D2C) Mesh** — an integrated platform that transforms disaster response from centralized, economically unsustainable relief into a decentralized local economy booster. The platform fuses three AI-powered tracks: (1) hyperlocal flood early warning using existing public data APIs and AI elevation models, (2) vehicle-aware dynamic routing that accounts for real-time flood depth, and (3) a decentralized E-Voucher system that lets government/donors inject funds directly to victims, redeemable at nearby safe SMEs acting as "Micro-Relief Stations." The platform solves the "built and abandoned" syndrome by transitioning into a year-round Community Commerce & Local Welfare Hub during non-disaster seasons. Pilot region: Nakhon Pathom & Bangkok perimeter (Tha Chin river basin). [source: participant]

## 6. Customer Journey — Current State

**Scenario: Seasonal flooding hits a residential area in Nakhon Pathom**

1. **⚠️ Warning phase** — Residents receive generic province-level flood warnings from government channels (TV, LINE official). No street-level or timing specificity. Many ignore warnings due to "cry wolf" fatigue or uncertainty about whether *their* street will flood. [source: participant]

2. **🚗 Evacuation / Movement** — Residents and rescue teams use standard navigation apps (Google Maps, HERE) that do not detect real-time flood depths on local roads. Vehicles get stuck in unexpectedly flooded streets. Rescue teams waste hours on impassable routes. Lifted pickups that *could* pass are routed the same as sedans. [source: participant]

3. **📦 Supply access** — Survival kits are procured and distributed through centralized government channels. Distribution is slow (days), bottlenecked at logistics hubs, and often mismatched to actual needs. Meanwhile, local grocery stores and markets in non-flooded zones sit idle with available stock but zero customers and zero revenue. [source: participant]

4. **💸 Economic impact** — SMEs lose all revenue during flood periods. Post-disaster, many small businesses cannot recover. Government relief funds flow through bureaucratic channels, not directly into the local economy. [source: participant]

5. **📱 Post-disaster** — Any flood-related apps or platforms built during the crisis are abandoned after waters recede because they have no daily-use value proposition. The cycle repeats next flood season with no institutional memory or infrastructure retained. [source: participant]

## 7. Supporting Agent Notes

- **aidlc-architect-agent (feasibility signal):** The three-track integration (early warning → routing → commerce) creates significant architectural complexity. The software-first approach for Track 6 is sound in principle but requires validation of API data granularity. The E-Voucher system must handle concurrent high-load redemptions during peak crisis periods. Offline/degraded-connectivity mode is likely a hard architectural requirement given flood-zone conditions. [source: system assessment]
